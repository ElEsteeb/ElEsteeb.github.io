Many times, when we read Paul, we wonder at the Spirit-inspired wisdom that he displays. Yet Paul himself rejects the urge to say more than he can understand. There has been much discussion about what Paul is talking about in this passage, but God has revealed enough for Paul to proclaim His purposes faithfully, while the depths of God's wisdom remain beyond Paul's ability to exhaust.

Paul has a problem he has been wrestling with since the beginning of the letter. Throughout the entirety of the letter, he has been showing how no one is innocent with regard to their sin before God. He also shows that salvation comes only through God's active grace, going so far as to reiterate that God is the one who chooses whom and how He saves. Here Paul is left with the question, "What do we make of all the promises God made to Israel?"

He calls back to the concept of God hardening people. This reminds us of God hardening Pharaoh in Romans 9. God says that it was in order that "I might show my power in you, and that my name might be proclaimed in all the earth" (Romans 9:17). We've already seen that God's hardening does not frustrate His purposes. Pharaoh's hardening displayed God's power; here Israel's partial hardening has accompanied salvation going to the Gentiles.

Christians have disagreed about precisely what Paul means when he says "all Israel will be saved." That question is larger than this devotional. But whatever interpretation we adopt must account for what Paul says about the Deliverer and salvation from sin.

He continues by looking at Isaiah, where God promises a Deliverer who will "banish ungodliness from Jacob" and "take away their sins" (Romans 11:26–27). Whatever else Paul means in this passage, we cannot understand this salvation apart from the Deliverer dealing with sin.

Paul finally reiterates something he has been saying all along: everyone comes to God from the same position. Everyone is disobedient; there is no innocent clay. "For God has consigned all to disobedience, that he may have mercy on all" (Romans 11:32). So, it is God who has mercy, not we who do or are anything worthy of salvation.

Finally, Paul exclaims, "Oh, the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are his judgments and how inscrutable his ways!" (Romans 11:33). Paul stops where we should all stop, at wonder. Paul does not claim to understand God's plan completely, and we should not make that claim either. God's plans are not something we are ever guaranteed to understand. Moses, in Deuteronomy 29:29, reminds us that the secret things of God are His alone.

When we study the story of God's amazing grace, we should humbly understand that we will never fully understand. Our faith is not in understanding God, but in believing Him. The Deliverer comes to deal with our ungodliness and take away our sins. We should patiently and humbly examine ourselves that we be those people.

We give thanks for the wondrous mystery of God's wisdom and seek not to be wise in our own eyes.

-steve
