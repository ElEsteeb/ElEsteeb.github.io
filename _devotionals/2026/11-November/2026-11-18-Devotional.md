title:  "The Shadow Is Not Greater Than the Substance"

It is our natural, sinful instinct to add something of our own doing to the work of salvation. We desire the satisfaction of believing that we have earned something by our own efforts. Likewise, we are naturally inclined to establish rules for others, even where God has not commanded.

Throughout Christian history, we have often struggled in our fellowship because of disagreements over matters where God has given liberty. Some have insisted upon observing Old Testament dietary customs and holy days. Others have concluded that setting our minds on heavenly things requires withdrawing physically from society. We have developed systems of spiritual maturity and established rules concerning foods, pastimes, and hobbies that Christians supposedly must avoid.

Certainly, Scripture clearly identifies what is sinful. Yet we sometimes take God's commandments and expand them according to our own preferences, elevating personal convictions into obligations for everyone else.

Paul addresses practices associated with the Old Covenant, but the principle of his warning reaches further. We do not need an ever-expanding collection of regulations in order to know God. We know God through Christ Jesus. We do not need another program to reconcile us to God. We have Jesus. We do not need some new revelation of God's character. We have Jesus. And though books, teachings, and instruction can be useful, none can supply something lacking in Christ or become a requirement for drawing near to God.

In the most practical sense, Jesus is enough. He is greater than the shadows that pointed toward Him, greater than outward displays of asceticism, and greater than the appearance of holiness produced by self-imposed religion. True spiritual growth comes from holding fast to Christ, the Head, from whom the whole body is nourished and knit together.

We are free in Christ, and as we grow in knowing Him, we grow in knowing God. Let us not allow others to impose their personal regulations upon our conscience, nor seek recognition through public displays of religious devotion. As Jesus taught, those who practice righteousness merely to be seen by others have already received their reward.

Instead, let us walk faithfully before the Father who sees in secret, holding fast to Christ, in whom we have everything necessary for life and godliness.

**The shadow is not greater than the Substance.**

-steve
