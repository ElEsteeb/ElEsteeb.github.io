There is no sweeter comfort than the calm that follows the completion of something we have worked hard to accomplish. Whether graduating from school, completing a project, preparing a festive meal, or finishing a demanding physical exercise, the greater the effort, the greater the satisfaction and thankfulness when the work is finished.

As Paul presents Christ to us as the One we have received, he calls us to walk in the satisfaction of what Christ has accomplished. Unlike the satisfaction we experience from our own achievements, this is a satisfaction rooted in the completed work of another. Our faith in Christ, the fullness of God's revelation, produces in us an ever-growing thankfulness, not simply for what He has promised for our future, but for what He has already accomplished and continues to accomplish in us.

Paul warns us again against empty philosophy and deceit based on human traditions. He is not condemning philosophical thinking itself, for he frequently employs carefully reasoned arguments in proclaiming the gospel. Rather, he warns against systems of thought that are grounded merely in human wisdom rather than in Christ.

And the reason we should not be captivated by these philosophies is that Christ is enough. The Christian life is not about knowing Christ plus some other secret, experiencing some extraordinary emotion, or attaining some additional spiritual revelation. It is entirely and completely about knowing Christ. If we truly know Him, His love, His majesty, His will, and His ways, we have all that is necessary for life with God. There is nothing lacking in Christ that something else must supply.

The fullness of deity dwells bodily in Christ, and in Him we have been filled. We are utterly insufficient in ourselves, but in Christ we are fully supplied. Christ is the One through whom all things were created, the One in whom all things hold together, and the One in whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden. Now Paul tells us that we are filled in Him. The One who lacks nothing has made us His own.

We do not need Christ plus something else. We need Christ, and in Him we have all that we need.

**Christ is enough.**
