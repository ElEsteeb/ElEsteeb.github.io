As Paul reminds us that we have been raised with Christ, he gives us a remarkably practical application, though it may not seem so at first glance.

Paul's argument is wonderfully straightforward: If you have been raised to new life in Christ, then you belong to Him. Therefore, your thinking should not be governed by the things of this earth, but by the things above, where Christ is seated at the right hand of God. For if you are in Christ, your life is bound to the One who reigns over all things.

Paul reminds us that although we still live here on earth, there is a fullness of our life that has not yet been revealed. We still need to eat and sleep, work and play, feast and rest. We still experience the ordinary joys and sorrows of earthly life. Yet we no longer belong to this world as we once did. Our lives are hidden with Christ in God.

This changes how we face the circumstances around us. When the world is consumed by anxiety over earthly affairs, we remember that our lives are secure in Christ. When people despair because they cannot find significance in their accomplishments, we remember that our identity is found in Him. When disaster strikes, we may grieve and suffer, but we are not without hope, because our lives are hidden in Christ. And when the world celebrates political victories or financial prosperity as though these were the greatest possible triumphs, we remember that our greatest treasure is already ours in Him.

Our confidence does not rest upon the stability of nations, the strength of economies, the approval of others, or even our own ability to persevere. It rests upon Christ, who is seated at the right hand of God.

Who can ascend into heaven and remove us from Him? Who can overturn His reign or separate us from His love? The One who holds our lives is the very One who rules over all creation.

Therefore, whether we are tempted by the pleasures of this world or troubled by its sorrows, we must remember that neither its greatest rewards nor its deepest sufferings can compare with the glory that will be revealed in us. We do not abandon our earthly responsibilities, but we fulfill them with our minds set upon heavenly things, knowing that Christ Himself is our life.

And when He appears in glory, the life that is now hidden in Him will be revealed with Him.

**Until then, we walk upon the earth with our lives hidden in heaven.**
